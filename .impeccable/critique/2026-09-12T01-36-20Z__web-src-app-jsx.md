## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Authored, not interchangeable — with one interchangeable organ transplanted in. The flag, the "In Memoriam" kicker, typewriter datelines, the oxblood-only accent, hairline-ruled rows under date headings, and a colophon with a phone number could only be a small newspaper's obituary page. The exception is the browse panel: a boxed faceted-filter block (14 month chips with counts, a 26-letter strip, two selects) that would be at home in any e-commerce sidebar. And the search field, which PRODUCT.md calls the product, is plainer than the surface around it deserves.

**Deterministic scan:** CLI scan of web/index.html, App.jsx, components/, index.css, main.jsx: clean (0 findings; fonts stylesheet is a build-time placeholder so the static pass covers project CSS only). In-page detector: 40 items — 6 × undersized functional text at 10.5px (tagline, sponsor label, the four browse labels), 32 × "all-caps body" (every row's funeral-home caption), 1 × eyebrow chip above the H1 ("In Memoriam"), 2 × line length ~112 chars, 1 × cream page background. Caught what the review skimmed: the four browse labels at 10.5px are the smallest functional text on the page. False positives: the 32 all-caps hits are tracked mono captions (the system's label voice); the "In Memoriam" eyebrow is a documented brand commitment; the cream ground is the committed newsprint world.

**Agreement:** every text color passes AA (muted-on-newsprint 4.81:1, faint 5.01:1, oxblood 8.17:1, white-on-oxblood 9.13:1). Review-only findings: the search field's border is 1.47:1 against its fill and chip borders 1.34:1 (non-text contrast); horizontal overflow at a true 375px viewport (Funeral-home select right edge at 385px) — the pane emulates 386px so B could not confirm.

## Overall Impression

The surface looks like exactly what it is — a local newsroom's memorial page — and the row-level craft is the best thing on it. But the register is organized around what the newsroom does (publish batches, sell a sponsorship, take submissions) rather than what the searcher does (type a name). The search field is the tenth element on the page, below the fold on desktop and phone, and then fails the most natural query form for most of the catalogue. Biggest opportunity: make the name search the first thing the reader meets and make it forgiving.

## What's Working

1. The register row: ruled portrait, serif name at 700, typewriter lifespan, one summary sentence, funeral home as a quiet caption; whole row one link with an inset oxblood focus ring; long names wrap cleanly.
2. Interaction plumbing: focus into/out of the form, "Show earlier" appends without a scroll jump and announces the count, aria-hidden skeleton with sr-only announcement, reduced motion honored in CSS and JS, manual carousel choice stops auto-advance.
3. The masthead as provenance: seal, wordmark, tagline, thick-over-thin rule, "In Memoriam", a lede that says it's free and when it publishes, a colophon with a phone number.

## Priority Issues

- **[P0] First-plus-last-name search returns nothing for most people.** App.jsx matches with includes() over a joined string; "Allan Jensen" ≠ "Allan Guy Jensen"; 89% of records have ≥3 name tokens; "jensen" returns 5 "names" of which 2 are Jensens, unranked. Fix: tokenize; every query token a prefix of a name token (strip quotes/punctuation, fold diacritics); rank name matches above summary/excerpt. Command: /impeccable harden
- **[P1] The search field is the tenth thing on the page and the faintest control on it.** Input top at 980px desktop / 1,112px phone; border 1.47:1; label 11px tracked caps. Fix: search directly under the lede, before sponsors and carousel; 2px ink/muted border (≥3:1), 17–18px serif text, glyph, sentence-case label; drop the masthead email paragraph; move "Submit an obituary" to the footer. Command: /impeccable layout
- **[P1] The browse panel exposes 39 controls on every load, sorts letter results by date, and overflows on phones.** 14 month chips → 6 lines at 375px; panel 395px tall; A–Z results in date order; Funeral-home select 10px past a true 375px viewport; labels 10.5px. Fix: collapse browse behind one disclosure (closed unless a filter is active); sort/group by surname when a letter is active; full-width stacked selects on phones; labels 11px. Command: /impeccable distill
- **[P2] Date headings are ambiguous and rows lack a confirmable fact line.** Publication-date headings read as death dates; 34% of records have no town; death date lives inside prose. Fix: label the heading ("Published September 2, 2026"); render death date and town as the typewriter line under the name. Command: /impeccable clarify
- **[P2] Dead ends land on the sponsor card; the carousel has no pause control.** No-results line followed by 80px sponsor logos with nothing to act on; carousel auto-advances with no visible pause and no aria-live. Fix: action row in the empty state (Clear search · Browse by last name · Not listed? Submit); "Browse the full index" above the sponsor card; a visible Pause beside the dots. Command: /impeccable onboard

## Minor Observations

- .browse uses a 4px radius; everything else is 2px per DESIGN.md.
- browse__count dims an 11px number with opacity .7 — use a color token.
- Register summary at Merriweather 300/16px; 400 would serve the confirming sentence better.
- The featured carousel disappears permanently once any filter is touched.
- Row hover underlines the name in oxblood; the featured card doesn't.
- Some vendored "portraits" are funeral-home stock placeholders (a white dove on sky) rendered as photographs; detect known placeholder assets in photos.py and let the monogram tile take over.
- Summary line length ~85–112 chars at 720px; a 65ch cap would help.
- No URL state for query or filter.

## Questions to Consider

- If the name search is the product, why does the page open with everything except the search — and would the sponsors lose anything if their logos moved below the results?
- Who is the A–Z row for? If "nobody in particular," the browse panel could be one line.
- The register groups by publication date because the batch posts do. Is that the reader's calendar or the newsroom's?
